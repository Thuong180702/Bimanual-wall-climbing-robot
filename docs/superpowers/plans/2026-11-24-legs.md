# Legs and the Full Robot Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Draw two G1-class 6-DOF legs and the arm upgrades that let the arms still hang the whole robot, as a separate "full" robot variant, and prove it with load, clearance and lidar checks.

**Architecture:** A switch in `cad/src/lib/spec.py` (`ROBOT`, from the environment variable `ROBOT`, default `"v2"`) adds the legs' joints, the RobStride 04 hip/knee and the upgraded arm actuators only when `ROBOT=full`. Every existing generator keeps writing the v2 files byte-for-byte by default; with `ROBOT=full` they write into `urdf/full/` and `cad/STEP/robot_full.step`. One new check script, `cad/tools/check_full.py`, holds every acceptance check of the design as asserts.

**Tech Stack:** Python 3, cadgen/build123d (CAD), MuJoCo (FK, torque, inverse dynamics, distances), the repo's existing tools (`check_one.py`, `check_pair.py`, `check_fit.py`, `torque.py`, `verify.py`, `massprops.py`, `gen_urdf.py`, `urdf/_gen_scene.py`, `urdf/_gen_keyframe.py`).

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-24-legs-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- Every leg joint and every arm joint: >= **1.3x peak torque** over its load cases, at the final mass of the full robot.
- Leg actuators: hip pitch and knee **RobStride 04** (120 N.m peak, 40 N.m rated, 200 rpm no-load, 1.42 kg, 48 V, CAN); hip roll **AK80-64**; hip yaw **AK60-39**; ankle **2 x AK60-39** on the calf driving ankle pitch + roll through two rods.
- Lengths: thigh (hip pitch axis to knee axis) **0.320 m**, calf (knee to ankle axis) **0.320 m**, ankle axis to sole **0.060 m**; foot about **0.20 x 0.09 m**, rubber sole.
- Arm targets at ~43 kg (to be measured): elbow need ~200 N.m peak, shoulder roll ~137, shoulder rotation ~130, wrist flexion ~110, wrist deviation ~76, forearm roll ~67, finger ~38; shoulder flexion keeps AK80-64.
- `ROBOT` unset or `v2`: `urdf/climbing_robot.urdf`, `urdf/climbing_robot_floating.urdf`, `urdf/_actuators.json`, `urdf/scene.xml` byte-identical to today; the training robot, lessons, `rl/`, fixtures and `paper/` are not touched.
- CAD connector workflow: **one connector at a time, rendered, confirmed by the user before the next**; a plate is exactly its motor face, a hole for every bolt of that face, webs follow both rims, no wrapping a motor.
- Local compute: at most 60 % CPU (~7 of 12 cores), one heavy process at a time, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` for single-threaded jobs.
- Commits only after the user okays them; work on a branch, never `main`; never commit `paper/`.

## Review Focus

1. **v2 regression:** running any generator with `ROBOT` unset after these changes must leave the v2 files identical (Task 1 test, re-run in Task 13).
2. **Right leg = mirror of the left:** a right-leg part drawn separately drifts; right links are `mirrored()` like the arms, and FK of the right sole is the left's with y negated (Task 2 test).
3. **Ankle torque through the rods:** the ankle joints have no motor at the joint; their capacity is the two calf motors through the linkage's Jacobian, and it must hold over the whole ankle range, not just at zero (Task 7 test).
4. **Stowed legs vs the lidars:** a stow pose that clears every wall but hides a rung from the chest lidar breaks the climbing method (Task 10 test).
5. **Arm speed after any added stage:** a stage that passes torque can fail the swings' joint speed (Task 12 dynamic test).

### Tasks 3-8: The leg's parts, one connector at a time

Each task draws ONE builder in `cad/src/lib/legs.py` (new; registered in `build.BUILDERS` only when `ROBOT == "full"`; right links via `build.mirrored`), and follows the same five steps:

- [ ] **Step 1: Run** `ROBOT=full python cad/tools/check_one.py <link>` -> FAIL (no builder).
- [ ] **Step 2: Implement** the builder named below, with the connector rules of the Global Constraints (each plate exactly its motor face, a hole for every bolt of it, webs follow both rims; parts named so `collide.col_parts` gives one hull each).
- [ ] **Step 3: Run** `ROBOT=full python cad/tools/check_one.py <link>` and `ROBOT=full python cad/tools/check_pair.py` -> no interference, every bolt hole on its face.
- [ ] **Step 4: Render** `cadgen step snapshot <link step> <png> --camera "0:0"` and `--camera "45:20"` with its two actuators; send the images to the user and **stop until they confirm**.
- [ ] **Step 5: Commit** (after the user's okay).

| Task | link builder | joins |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | `base_link` (full) | the pelvis hip walls redrawn for the RS04 hip pitch (its front face and bolt circle, `IFACE["RS04"]`), keeping the waist mounting and the harness windows |
| 4 | `hip_L` | RS04 hip pitch output -> AK80-64 hip roll housing |
| 5 | `hip_roll_L` | AK80-64 roll output -> AK60-39 hip yaw housing |
| 6 | `thigh_L` | AK60-39 yaw output -> RS04 knee housing, 0.320 m |
| 7 | `calf_L` | RS04 knee output -> the two ankle AK60-39 (both faces bolted), their cranks, the two rods (with rod-end ball joints, drawn as purchased-part envelopes), the ankle cross's bearings at 0.320 m |
| 8 | `ankle_L`, `foot_L` | the ankle cross (pitch then roll), the foot plate 0.20 x 0.09 m with the rods' lower ball joints and a separate rubber sole part, ankle axis 0.060 m over the sole, about 40 % from the heel |

Task 7 adds `check_ankle(need_pitch=50.6, need_roll=19.0)` to `check_full.py` before its Step 2: over a 5 deg grid of the ankle range, the ankle torque the two motors can give through the rods (`J^T` of the drawn linkage, each motor at 72 N.m peak) is >= 1.3x `need_pitch` and >= 1.3x `need_roll`, and no rod is within 10 deg of a singular (crank and rod in line). The defaults are one-leg stance at 43 kg with the COM over the toe (0.12 m ahead of the ankle axis) and over the foot's edge (0.045 m); Task 11 calls it again with the measured needs.
