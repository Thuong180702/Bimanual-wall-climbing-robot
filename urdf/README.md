# The robot

17 real CubeMars AK-series actuators, and every mass, inertia, envelope and
torque limit traced to either a datasheet or the CAD solids. Nothing here is a
plausible-looking number.

Everything in this directory except `climbing_env.py` is GENERATED from
[`../cad/`](../cad/README.md). Do not hand-edit.

    python cad/tools/gen_urdf.py     # links, meshes, joints, _actuators.json
    python urdf/_gen_scene.py        # scene.xml: walls, rungs, actuators, sensors
    python urdf/_gen_keyframe.py --write     # settle and verify the hang
    python cad/tools/verify.py       # 18 checks, source by source
    python -m rl.lessons             # the curriculum scenes

The robot this replaced - aluminium cylinders with masses and `effort=` values
derived from geometry, 11.13 kg, fine in simulation and impossible to build -
is in git history at the commit before this one.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `climbing_robot.urdf` | fixed base |
| `climbing_robot_floating.urdf` | free base - **use this one for MuJoCo** |
| `scene.xml` | the corridor, the robot, 17 actuators, 86 sensors, a verified hanging keyframe |
| `scene_wide_shoulders.xml` | the same with abduction opened 60 deg outward |

## The actuators

Three part numbers, one vendor, one CAN protocol, one driver family. All three
are high-ratio planetary units, not quasi-direct-drive — a climbing robot spends
its life holding a static load, which is what gear ratio buys and what QDD
gives away.

| | AK80-64 KV80 | AK60-39 V3.0 KV80 | AK45-36 KV80 |
|---|---|---|---|
| peak / rated torque | 120 / 48 N·m | 72 / 24 N·m | 24 / 8 N·m |
| no-load / rated speed | 7.85 / 5.03 rad/s | 10.3 / 7.33 rad/s | 5.45 / 4.19 rad/s |
| gear ratio | 64:1 | 39:1 | 36:1 |
| mass | 850 g | 750 g | 340 g |
| envelope | Ø98 × 61.9 mm | Ø79 × 67 mm | Ø55 × 54 mm |
| encoder | 14-bit | dual 21-bit | 14-bit |
| backlash | 0.18° | 15 arcmin | 12 arcmin |
| supply | 24/48 V | 48 V | 24 V |
| used at | 8 joints | 8 joints | 2 joints |

Sources: [AK80-64](https://www.cubemars.com/product/ak80-64-kv80-robotic-actuator.html),
[AK60-39](https://www.cubemars.com/product/ak60-39-v3-0-kv80-robotic-actuator.html),
[AK45-36](https://www.cubemars.com/product/AK45-36.html),
[AK series driver manual](https://store.tmotor.com/images/file/202205/311653967459529218.pdf).
Geometry: the vendor's own STEP files from [step.parts](https://www.step.parts),
sha256-verified, and their envelopes measured back off the files agree with the
datasheets to 0.1 mm (`cad/tools/verify.py`).

**Control interface** is the same for all three: CAN at 1 Mbps, with both a
servo mode (dual-loop, more accurate position and torque) and MIT mode
(single-loop position/velocity/torque with Kp/Kd, the interface a learned
policy wants). One bus, one driver library, 17 node IDs. The host command rate
is not quoted by the vendor and needs measuring on the bench — `scene.xml`
steps at 500 Hz, and if the bus cannot hold that the policy's action rate is
what has to change, not the physics timestep.

**Speed is not the binding constraint.** v1 assumed `velocity=3.0` rad/s
everywhere; the slowest of these three does 5.45 rad/s no-load. The 48 V rail is
what makes that true — AK60-39 at 24 V would be too slow.

**The mixed 24 V / 48 V rail is a real cost.** The two AK45-36 need a buck
converter off the 48 V pack. It buys 820 g at the far end of the arm, which is
worth more than the converter costs.

## Sizing: measured, not assumed

v1's `effort=` values came from "the whole robot hangs off this joint's distal
chain at maximum reach". That is the right model for a **free** arm and the
wrong one for a hanging robot, and it got the answer backwards: it put 68 N·m at
the shoulder and 13 N·m at the finger, when the shoulder is close to the load
line and the wrist is the joint the whole body dangles from.

`cad/tools/torque.py` measures instead. A body hanging off one hook is a
pendulum, so it rotates about the anchor until its COM is straight below. For
each joint, cut the robot there; whichever side does not contain the anchor is
the load; the hold torque is the moment of that load about the joint axis. Two
requirement sets come out of it:

- **full joint box** — the pose swept anywhere the limits allow
- **climbing envelope** — the hook only latches with the arm raised (v1's own
  finding), so shoulder flexion −170…−70°, elbow −90…0°, abduction ±45°

| joint | actuator | peak | rated | need (box) | need (env) | margin box | margin env |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| waist_yaw | AK60-39 | 72 | 24 | 0.0 | 0.0 | — | — |
| shoulder_abduction | AK80-64 | 120 | 48 | 60.7 | 46.2 | 1.98× | 2.60× |
| shoulder_flexion | AK80-64 | 120 | 48 | 74.4 | 31.4 | 1.61× | 3.83× |
| shoulder_rotation | AK80-64 | 120 | 48 | 67.8 | 50.0 | 1.77× | 2.40× |
| elbow | AK80-64 | 120 | 48 | 82.6 | 63.6 | **1.45×** | 1.89× |
| forearm_roll | AK60-39 | 72 | 24 | 26.5 | 23.5 | 2.72× | 3.06× |
| wrist_flexion | AK60-39 | 72 | 24 | 45.7 | 39.9 | 1.58× | 1.80× |
| wrist_deviation | AK60-39 | 72 | 24 | 29.6 | 26.4 | 2.43× | 2.72× |
| finger | AK45-36 | 24 | 8 | 14.7 | 14.7 | 1.63× | 1.63× |

Margins are against PEAK torque, which is the right test for a hold that lasts
seconds. Against the CONTINUOUS rating the elbow, shoulder rotation and both
wrist joints are over - the elbow wants 63.6 N·m where the AK80-64 is rated 48.
A hang that has to last minutes needs either a brake or the next size up; that
is a decision, not an oversight, and it is the same one v1's numbers implied.

Three things this table says that are worth keeping:

1. **waist_yaw carries no gravity moment at all.** Its axis is vertical and
   gravity is vertical. It is sized for turning the body's inertia against the
   torso-on-wall friction, not for holding anything, and 72 N·m is generous.
2. **The elbow is the most loaded joint, not the shoulder** — lever 0.381 m at
   the hang equilibrium versus 0.347 m at the shoulder. The elbow sticks out
   sideways from the load line; the shoulder sits near it.
3. **Continuous holding is not the binding case.** Resting on two hands halves
   the load, and a well-postured rest drives it near zero, so the rated column
   only has to cover the two-hand hang: worst is wrist_flexion at 21.0 N·m
   against 24 N·m rated. The single-hand reach is seconds at a time and is sized
   against peak.

**What a thin margin actually costs.** If the elbow cannot hold a pose, the arm
sags toward a lower-torque configuration — the robot does not fall, it just
loses that pose. The joints that would drop the robot are the ones holding the
hook on the rung (finger 1.65×, wrist_flexion 1.71×, wrist_deviation 2.67×), and
those have the margin.

## What moved, and why

Every offset below differs from v1 because two real actuator bodies could not
occupy the same space. Two actuators on perpendicular axes clear each other only
if their axis lines are at least r₁ + r₂ + 3 mm apart, or their finite bodies
miss along the link. `cad/tools/check_fit.py` checks this on solids — the
analytic version got it wrong for perpendicular axes, which is why it is a
boolean intersection now.

| offset | v1 | v2 | forced by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `torso_upper → shoulder_abduction` z | 0.165 | **0.269** | two Ø98 actuators, perpendicular axes, need 101 mm |
| `shoulder → shoulder_flexion` z | 0 | **−0.104** | same pair, from the other side |
| `wrist → wrist_deviation` z | −0.020 | **−0.084** | two Ø79 actuators need 82 mm |
| `wrist_dev → finger` z | −0.020 | **−0.072** | Ø79 + Ø55 need 70 mm |
| torso_upper width | 0.240 | **0.145** | the Ø98 abduction actuator reaches in to y = 0.076 |
| lower torso width | 0.210 | **0.180**, corners rounded to R90 | the waist turns the arms over a *stationary* lower torso; the elbow actuator sweeps a circle of radius 93.1 mm, so nothing may stick out past 90 mm |

The first two cancel: the shoulder **flexion** axis is still at world z = 0.405,
exactly where v1 put it. **Everything between the shoulder and the wrist is
dimensionally identical to v1** — the torso grew 104 mm taller instead of the
arm changing. Only the wrist stack is longer, by 116 mm, because three actuators
in series need more than 40 mm.

Two joint limits also moved, both measured:

- **shoulder_flexion −180 → −170°.** Past −173 the rotation actuator grazes the
  abduction actuator (23 mm³ at −174, 526 mm³ at −180). v1's README already said
  not to go there: at −180 the torso hangs flat against the wall and rams the
  rung below.
- **elbow −120 → −118°.** At −120 the forearm folds far enough that the
  forearm-roll actuator touches the shoulder-rotation one, by 15 mm³.

And the shoulder abduction actuator slides **40 mm back along its own axis**
rather than sitting on the joint origin, so the raised arm's rotation actuator
sweeps past it. A joint axis is a line; sliding an actuator along it is
kinematically free, and it was cheaper than any geometric offset — growing the
shoulder offset made that particular clash *worse*, because it lifts the
abduction actuator into the sweep.

### One pose v1 used that the real machine cannot hold

v1's reach optimisation reported its best pose as abduction +180°, flexion
−177°, elbow −114°, and derived 0.671 m from it. That pose puts the upper arm
through the torso — 102 000 mm³ of it. v1 would have collided there too; its
optimiser simply did not check. `_reach.py` enforces self-collision, and also
requires the COM to sit below the anchored hook (otherwise the "reach" is
measured from a hand the robot is standing on, which is how an early run of it
reported a nonsense 1.143 m). With both constraints the answer is 0.676 m, measured on this model.

## Sim2real: three numbers that are estimates, not datasheet

Everything else here is traceable. These three are not, and they are the first
things to identify on hardware.

**`armature` — reflected rotor inertia, N² · J_rotor.** v1 used `armature="0.01"`
for all 17 joints. A 64:1 AK80-64 reflects about **0.209 kg·m²** into its joint,
twenty times that, and more than any link's own inertia. A 36:1 AK45-36 reflects
0.0083. Getting this wrong makes a geared joint feel massless in simulation and
sluggish on the bench; it is the single largest sim2real term added here.
J_rotor itself is modelled as a disc holding 20 % of the actuator's mass at half
its outer radius, calibrated so an AK80-9 lands at the 2.4 × 10⁻³ kg·m² people
measure on that unit. **Identify it before trusting any transient.**

**`frictionloss`** is scaled from the one published backdrive figure (AK45-36,
0.8 N·m at 36:1) by gear ratio. Also to be identified.

**Actuator mass is booked entirely to the parent link.** The housing is bolted
to the parent and the rotor turns with the child; the rotor is a small fraction
of the whole, so the error is bounded by that fraction, and its inertia is in
`armature` where it belongs.

Two more honest caveats: the actuator's inertia tensor is a uniform cylinder of
its catalogue mass (the vendor STEP has no materials, so its volume cannot give
a mass), and the visual meshes are tessellated at 0.6 mm — exact geometry lives
in `cad/STEP/robot.step`.

## Verified

`python cad/tools/verify.py` — all 18 checks pass.

- Three vendor STEP files match their step.parts sha256, and their envelopes
  measured off the files match the datasheets: 61.9 × 98 × 98, 67 × 79 × 79,
  54 × 55 × 55 mm.
- All three canonicalised to +Z = joint axis, output flange at z = 0.
- Link masses sum to structure + actuators + payload = 23.7369 kg, and MuJoCo
  agrees to 1 mg.
- All 19 inertia tensors positive definite and obeying the triangle inequality.
- All 17 joints: axis, range and forcerange match spec and datasheet.
- Torque margin ≥ 1.35× peak over the entire joint box, ≥ 1.65× over the
  climbing envelope.
- No two actuator bodies intersect in any legal named pose (8 poses tested,
  including the keyframe, waist hard over while hanging, and elbows fully flexed).
- **Keyframe `hang_on_rung`**: both hooks latched on `rung_front_line_0`, servos
  holding. The pendulum takes ~20 s to stop swinging; after that the base drifts
  **0.10 mm per 5 s** and the replay holds 12 contacts. Hand forces 161.7 +
  114.5 N against a 232.9 N robot, the balance through the wrists resting on the
  wall.
- **Grip closed to `GRIP_SET` (−25°)**: holds 12 s untouched, `latched` and
  `grip_ok` true on both hands, no termination — v1's `climbing_env.py` runs
  against this scene unchanged.

Not checked: fastener stress, thermal duty cycle, wiring routing, stiffness
under load. The 3 mm wall thickness is set by handling and local buckling, not
stress — a 70 × 40 × 3 box section sees 15 MPa at 120 N·m and 6061-T6 yields at
276 — but no FEA was run.

## Regenerating

`meshes/` and `cad/STEP/` are not in git (18 MB and 31 MB of regenerable STL
and STEP), and neither is `cad/vendor/` — those files are the manufacturers'.
From a fresh clone:

```bash
python cad/tools/fetch_vendor.py   # 3 vendor STEP files, sha256 checked
python cad/src/robot.py            # the CAD assembly (STEP)
python cad/tools/gen_urdf.py       # URDF + meshes from the CAD
python urdf/_gen_scene.py         # scene.xml (drops the keyframe)
python urdf/_gen_keyframe.py --write   # find and verify the keyframe, write it back
python cad/tools/verify.py         # check everything
```

Analysis, any time:

```bash
python cad/tools/check_fit.py --sweep 2000   # self-intersection, named poses + random
python cad/tools/torque.py 25000 urdf/scene.xml out.json [--envelope]
python urdf/_reach.py 60000                 # vertical reach, self-collision enforced
```
